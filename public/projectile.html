Projectile motion, from the ground up

Two ordinary 1-D motions at once: constant vx, and vy that gravity steadily changes.

Two independent motions

vx = v0 cos θ   (constant)
vy = v0 sin θ − g t

Range peaks at 45°

R = v0² sin(2θ) / g
H = v0² sin²θ / (2g)

Predicted graphs for these example conditions

v0 = 20 m/s · g = 9.8 m/s² · no air resistance · equal launch and landing height

Launch Angle vs. Horizontal Range

R(θ) = v₀² sin(2θ) / g. The peak is at 45°. 30° and 60° sit at the same height on this curve.

Launch Angle vs. Maximum Height

H(θ) = v₀² sin²(θ) / (2g). Height keeps rising toward 90°, the opposite of the range graph.

Fit your recorded trials

The graphs above are the exact identities. Your Lab graphs are only the trials you recorded. Turn on Show exact identity to overlay the closed-form curve. Range should peak at 45°; height should keep rising toward 90°.
